Back v lay

How the bets and trackers are scored

Back means betting the selection will win. A £5 back at 1.60 makes £3 if it wins and loses £5 if not.

Lay means betting the selection will not win, on the exchange. A £5 lay at 4.80 wins £5 if the selection fails to win and loses £19 (5 × 3.80) if it wins.

Accumulators combine the day's top back picks. Every leg must win, so the chances multiply down quickly. A void leg drops out. Accas are placed with a bookmaker, so real odds will be a little lower than shown.

The wild card is one bigger-priced horse a day from racing anywhere in the world, backed £5 each-way (£10 in total). Half wins if it wins; the other half pays a fraction of the odds (usually a quarter or a fifth) if it finishes in the places. So a 14/1 shot placing still turns a profit, and a winner pays big. Expect plenty of losing days between hits.

The No.1 bank starts with £10 and puts the whole bank on each day's No.1 pick (for a lay, the bank is the amount at risk). Winnings roll into the next day. If a pick loses, the bank is gone; a fresh £10 goes in the next day and the tracker counts how much has been put in overall.

All profit is before exchange commission (usually 2–5% of winnings).
