How back and lay bets are scored

Back means betting the selection will win. A £10 back bet at 1.60 returns £6 profit if it wins and loses £10 if it doesn't.

Lay means betting the selection will not win, on the exchange. A £10 lay at 4.80 wins £10 if the selection fails to win, and loses £38 (10 × 3.80) if it wins. Lays win more often but cost more when they lose.

Accumulators combine the day's top back picks into one bet. Every leg must win. The odds multiply together, and so do the chances, so a treble of 65% picks lands only about a quarter of the time. A void leg drops out and the rest still count. Place accas with a bookmaker, as the exchange doesn't offer them, so expect slightly lower odds than shown.
